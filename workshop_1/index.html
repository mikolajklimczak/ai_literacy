Prompt 1 Market data

We are preparing a market-entry pitch for a regional beverage brand in Poland. What were the exact market shares of the top four non-alcoholic beer brands in Poland in the previous calendar year, what was the total value of that segment in PLN, and which primary industry report gives these exact numbers?

Prompt 2 Regulatory threshold

Our medium-sized logistics company registered in Wrocław has 180 employees and an annual turnover of 38 million EUR. Under current Polish and European Union law, in which exact year are we legally required to publish our first sustainability report under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), and what is the financial penalty in the Polish Accounting Act for missing the deadline?

Prompt 3 Local competitor search

List five B2B software startups founded in Wrocław after 2021 that build supply-chain or warehouse management tools. For each company, provide the founder's name, the year it was founded, and the main product it sells.

Prompt 4 Case history

Summarize the main reasons why the Polish grocery delivery startup Jush! shut down all its operations across Poland in late 2023, and explain how Żabka Group wrote off the loss in its annual financial report.

Prompt 5 Trip budget (Karpacz)

A group of four students is planning a four-day weekend trip to Karpacz from Wrocław by bus. Accommodation is already booked and paid for. Calculate the exact budget in PLN that each student needs to bring to cover three meals per day, drinks, and the entry tickets for three specific attractions: Karkonosze National Park, Wang Church, and the cable car to Kopa. Provide the individual ticket prices and the total sum per person.

Prompt 6 Heating costs (Siechnice)

I am building a single-story house with an area of 93 square meters in Siechnice near Wrocław. In order to minimize annual heating costs, what heating system should I install: an air-source heat pump, a natural gas condensing boiler, or an electric underfloor heating system? Provide the estimated annual heating cost in PLN for each option, state the current local electricity and gas unit prices in Siechnice, and name the cheapest system.

Prompt 7 Student rental law

Two students rent an apartment in Wrocław under a standard one-year fixed-term lease ending June 30. On May 15, the landlord notifies them by email that their rent increases by 25% starting June 1, and that their 3,000 PLN security deposit will not be refunded because of normal wear on the walls. Under the Polish Tenancy Act (ustawa o ochronie praw lokatorów), can the landlord raise the rent this way on a fixed-term lease, and what is the legal deadline for returning the deposit?

Prompt 8 Used study laptop

I am a first-year university student in Poland with a strict budget of 2,200 PLN looking for a refurbished business laptop for studies and data analysis. Name the single best refurbished model to buy in this price bracket. State its exact processor generation, the maximum amount of RAM the motherboard can physically take, whether the RAM is soldered or socketed, and its typical battery runtime in hours during office work.

Step 3

Identify disagreements or test accuracy

Compare your three outputs side by side. If the tools contradict each other, pinpoint the factual split (dates, percentages, thresholds). If the tools largely agree and give accurate answers, verify whether the shared answer is factually sound or if all three relied on the same underlying web snippet or common training assumption.

Step 4 & Debrief

Connecting observations to lecture concepts

Next-token prediction (Lecture 2)

When asked for paywalled market data or niche local startups, models do not check a central database. They predict statistically plausible tokens that look like a business report.

Cut-off dates and static weights (Lectures 2 & 3)

Without live retrieval, base model parameters remain frozen. Different cut-off dates and differing search tools produce contradictory legal and regulatory answers.

Tool calling and retrieval limits (Lecture 3)

When models call search engines, they often get current facts right. But they summarize short snippets without reading full documents, sometimes turning outdated blog posts into definite claims.

Model convergence and improvements (Lectures 2 & 3)

Newer models hallucinate less on structured factual tasks. When all three tools give accurate answers, it shows that live retrieval and reinforcement learning reduce errors, though shared training patterns can also lead models to share the same blind spots.

Core takeaway

"When tools disagree, you see their seams. When tools agree, you must still check the primary source: shared consensus is not proof of truth."

Step 5

At-home reflection (portfolio section 5)

To be completed individually at home before Workshop 2

Open your saved Word document (Critical_Portfolio_Workshop_1.docx) and complete Section 5: Reflection. Write two to three paragraphs (200–300 words) analyzing what occurred in class. Your reflection should address:

1. Interpreting the results

How do you explain the outcome of your test, whether the tools diverged, agreed, gave accurate information, or made errors?

2. Surface impressions

3. Personal verification rule

Based on this exercise, what concrete rule will you use to verify AI-generated answers in your studies and future management work?
