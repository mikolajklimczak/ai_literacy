Step 1

Run your assigned prompt

Copy exactly without altering punctuation

You are assigned a number from 1 to 4. Find the matching prompt below, copy the text verbatim, and paste it into three different AI tools at the same time. Copy each full, unedited response into your Word Online document.

Prompt 1 Market data

We are preparing a market-entry pitch for a regional beverage brand in Poland. What were the exact market shares of the top four non-alcoholic beer brands in Poland in the previous calendar year, what was the total value of that segment in PLN, and which primary industry report gives these exact numbers?

Prompt 2 Regulatory threshold

Our medium-sized logistics company registered in Wrocław has 180 employees and an annual turnover of 38 million EUR. Under current Polish and European Union law, in which exact year are we legally required to publish our first sustainability report under the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD), and what is the financial penalty in the Polish Accounting Act for missing the deadline?

Prompt 3 Local competitor search

List five B2B software startups founded in Wrocław after 2021 that build supply-chain or warehouse management tools. For each company, provide the founder's name, the year it was founded, and the main product it sells.

Prompt 4 Case history

Summarize the main reasons why the Polish grocery delivery startup Jush! shut down all its operations across Poland in late 2023, and explain how Żabka Group wrote off the loss in its annual financial report.

Step 3

Identify the factual contradiction

Compare your three outputs side by side with your partner. Identify points where the tools directly contradict each other on objective data: a percentage, a legal year, a company name, a founder, or whether an event actually occurred.

Step 4 & Debrief

Connecting observations to lecture concepts

Next-token prediction (Lecture 2)

When asked for paywalled market data or niche local startups, models do not check a central database. They predict statistically plausible tokens that look like a business report.

Cut-off dates and static weights (Lectures 2 & 3)

Without live retrieval, base model parameters remain frozen. Different cut-off dates and differing search tools produce contradictory legal and regulatory answers.

Sycophancy and attention (Lectures 2 & 4)

When a prompt contains a false premise (such as an alleged company shutdown), the model frequently accepts the frame rather than questioning it, elaborating on false grounds.

Tool calling and retrieval limits (Lecture 3)

Even when search engines are called behind the scenes, models summarize brief snippets without reading full documents, often turning outdated press releases into definite claims.

Core takeaway

"You just watched three confident machines disagree about a fact. Confidence is not truth. Hold on to that for the rest of the course."
