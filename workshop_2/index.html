Step 2

The teardown and steering framework

A language model does not know your workplace, your budget, or your flatmates. When you type a ten-word prompt, the model calculates the most statistically probable words that follow those ten words across millions of internet documents. The statistical average of the internet is polite, vague filler. To steer the probability distribution, use the five building blocks from Lecture 5 alongside step-by-step reasoning.

1. Role and perspective

Specify who is speaking and who the audience is. Writing as a "student café manager in Wrocław addressing 6 part-time baristas" sets an immediate register and removes generic corporate phrasing.

2. Task and action

State the exact deliverable and what the reader must do after reading it (e.g., fill in a shift sheet by Friday at 16:00, approve one of three budget options).

3. Context and domain data

Supply every concrete number, date, name, and background cause from your scenario brief. Anything you omit will either be left out or hallucinated by the model.

4. Format and structure

Define how the text should look on the page: a comparison table with PLN columns, a day-by-day schedule, bulleted checklists, or a maximum word count.

5. Negative constraints

Tell the model what not to do: ban clichés ("I hope this email finds you well", "valued customer"), forbid inventing unstated policies, or cap the length at 180 words.

6. Step-by-step reasoning

For tasks involving math, budgets, or schedules, instruct the model to list the constraints and verify the arithmetic step by step before writing the final text.

Step 5

Debrief

Probability pruning (Lectures 2 & 5)

The model did not become smarter between Round 1 and Round 4. By supplying role, domain facts, format, and negative rules, you narrowed the probability distribution so generic internet templates were no longer high-probability completions.

Context length and attention dilution (Lecture 5)

When you stay in a single long chat thread across many corrections, the model's attention spreads across your old mistakes and rejected drafts. Consolidating your rules into a single clean prompt in a fresh chat prevents context pollution.

Session takeaway

"The quality of the answer is mostly decided before the AI says a word, by you."
