Rules of primary source verification

Asking an AI chatbot "Is this claim true?" is not verification. Models asked to check plausible fabrications frequently agree with the mistake or repeat outdated blog posts. You must verify claims using primary records: official statutory databases (isap.sejm.gov.pl, eur-lex.europa.eu), statistical offices (stat.gov.pl, ec.europa.eu/eurostat), regulators (uokik.gov.pl, podatki.gov.pl), or official municipal registries.

Step 4

Debrief: why fabricated claims bypass human scepticism

Class discussion

1. The illusion of decimal precision and authority

When an AI text states that 34.7% or 41.8% of companies failed an audit in a named GUS, PARP, or UOKiK report, human readers rarely suspect invention. Specific decimals and institutional acronyms trigger authority bias, even though a language model generates a fake report title with the exact same ease as a real one.

2. Regulatory mashups and half-truths

The hardest hallucinations to spot are rarely pure fantasy. Models frequently combine a real statute number with a real penalty rate from a different law or invent a plausible subparagraph (such as Art. 5(1)(z)). Checking only that the main act exists is insufficient; you must read the exact article text.

3. Temporal drift and zombie laws

Models trained on historical text—or search tools pulling top-ranked accounting blogs from 2022–2024—routinely present postponed deadlines (like the July 2024 KSeF rollout) or repealed laws (like the 20,000 PLN consumer cash limit) as binding current rules. Always check the effective date and consolidated text (tekst jednolity).

4. Geographic and US legal default bias

Because English-language American legal and corporate material dominates training corpora, models silently import US concepts—at-will employment, FTC disclosure tags, CARB emissions, FICO credit scores, and Title 17 Fair Use—into Polish and EU business memos.

Core takeaway

"You cannot trust a fact you have not checked against a primary record, and 'it sounded right' is not checking."
